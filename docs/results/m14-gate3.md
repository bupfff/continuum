# M14 gate 3: unique geometry beyond CPU memory, within a 60 fps budget

**PASS.** 4,096 unique rocks, whose finest levels hold 32× the camera
budget and 33 GB packed (more than the machine's 25.8 GB of memory), are
generated on demand by worker threads as the view needs them. Three
1,800-frame flights stay within a 60 fps frame budget (worst frame 14.5
ms), never over the GPU budget or the CPU cache's cap, and each settles
to within 0.018–0.030× one ray's coverage error of every rock at its
ideal level.

## Details

- **Scene:** 64 × 64 rocks 6 m apart (a 384 m square), gate 1's rocks and
  levels (finest 65,536 triangles at most; poles drop about 0.8%, so 31.7×
  and 33.0 GB with the actual counts). Lighting levels (about 1,000
  triangles each) resident, built in 12.2 s with the levels' errors; camera
  levels generated by four worker threads from each rock's shape when
  wanted. Camera budget 8 × 2²⁰ triangles; CPU cache 2 GiB; time budget
  2 ms of structure builds a frame at 30 ns a triangle.

## Method

Gate 2's streaming (paged pool, level chains, feedback), plus:

1. `LevelSource` (`InstancedScene::level_source`): levels whose surface is
   left empty (with a bound on their triangles) are produced on demand on
   worker threads (`StreamOptions::workers`), packed there, and kept in a
   CPU cache with a byte cap (`cpu_cache_bytes`), the least recently used
   dropped first. A wanted level not in CPU memory is requested and loaded
   once it arrives.
2. A streaming time budget (`StreamOptions::frame_ms`, at
   `build_ns_per_triangle`): structures built per frame stay within 2 ms;
   further wants wait (at least one level a frame). The user's decision
   (2026-11-12): streaming is measured against a 60 fps frame budget.
3. The reference: at each settled view, a renderer with every rock at its
   ideal level for the view (the coarsest within half a pixel at its
   nearest distance if in the view, computed on the CPU; its lighting
   level otherwise), all resident. Gates 1–2 measured ideal levels against
   the finest (0.06–0.10× one ray's error).

**Disclosed:** a development run (`M14_DEV=1`, 16 × 16 rocks, 300-frame
flights: worst frame 8.5 ms, settled 0.016–0.039×) and a CPU-only sizing of
this scene's ideal working set along the flights (`M14_SIZE=1`: at most
3.33 million triangles), both before the run.

## What the numbers say

- **Unique detail no longer has to fit anywhere at once:** neither the GPU
  (8.4 million camera triangles for 268 million) nor CPU memory (2 GiB of
  cache for 33 GB).
- **The time budget removed gate 2's spikes:** worst frames 8.7–14.5 ms
  against 29–34 ms, at the price of 384–926 deferred loads a flight;
  0.3–0.5% of seen rocks show a coarser level than wanted in flight.
- **What stays resident now limits scale:** the lighting levels (4,096 ×
  about 1,000 triangles, with hierarchies and structures) are most of the
  3.3 GB at creation, and grow with every unique object. Next (M14 gate
  4): stream the lighting level by cone footprint, certified.
